What Is DTG Printing?

Direct-to-Garment (DTG) printing is a digital printing technology that works similarly to a desktop inkjet printer — except instead of printing on paper, it prints directly onto fabric. The result is a soft, breathable print with photographic quality and virtually unlimited color reproduction.

Unlike screen printing, which requires physical screens to be made for each color, DTG reads your design digitally and prints it exactly as-is. This makes it ideal for complex artwork, photographs, gradients, and one-off pieces where traditional printing methods would be prohibitively expensive.

Key insight: DTG is the only printing method that can reproduce photographic images and complex artwork with unlimited colors at no extra cost — making it the go-to choice for modern, intricate designs.

How DTG Printing Works

The DTG process involves several carefully orchestrated steps:

  1. Pre-treatment: The garment is sprayed with a special pre-treatment solution that helps the ink bond to the fabric fibers. This step is critical for color accuracy and wash durability — especially on dark-colored garments.
  2. Loading: The treated garment is stretched flat onto a printer platen and loaded into the DTG machine. Positioning is precise to ensure accurate print placement.
  3. Printing: The printer head moves across the garment, depositing CMYK (and white ink for dark garments) in precise droplets at 600 DPI resolution.
  4. Curing: The garment passes through a heat press or tunnel dryer at ~330°F to permanently cure the ink into the fabric fibers.

Preparing Your Artwork for DTG

The quality of your DTG print is directly tied to the quality of your artwork file. Follow these guidelines to ensure the best possible result:

  • Resolution: Minimum 300 DPI at full print size. 600 DPI is ideal and matches our printer's output resolution.
  • File Formats: PNG (with transparent background preferred), AI, EPS, PDF, SVG. Avoid JPEG for artwork with fine details or text.
  • Color Mode: Use sRGB color profile. Avoid CMYK files — our RIP software converts them, but sRGB produces more accurate results.
  • Transparency: Use transparent backgrounds when printing on colored garments to prevent unwanted white areas around your design.
  • Text: Make sure all text is rasterized or outlined before submitting. Minimum readable font size is 8pt.

Best Fabrics for DTG Printing

DTG works best on natural fiber fabrics, particularly cotton. Here's how different fabric compositions affect print quality:

  • 100% Cotton: The gold standard for DTG. Ink bonds perfectly with cotton fibers for vivid colors and exceptional durability.
  • Cotton/Poly Blends: 60/40 or 50/50 blends produce good results. The higher the cotton content, the better the print quality.
  • Tri-Blends: Produce a slight "vintage" texture that many brands use intentionally for a soft, worn aesthetic.
  • 100% Polyester: DTG is not ideal for synthetic fabrics — sublimation is a better choice for polyester garments.

Pro tip: For your best results, always choose 100% ringspun cotton garments at 4.5–6.0 oz weight. Lighter shirts can show print through the back; heavier ones are sturdier but take longer to cure.

Printing on Dark vs. Light Colored Garments

One of the most common questions we get is about printing on dark-colored shirts. Here's what you need to know:

Light garments (white, cream, pastels): The simplest and most cost-effective DTG prints. CMYK inks print directly onto the fabric without needing a white underbase.

Dark garments (black, navy, dark colors): Require a white ink underbase to be printed first — essentially a white "canvas" for the colors to sit on. This adds a small cost (+$2/piece at PRINTCRAFT) but produces vivid, accurate colors on dark fabrics.

Wash Care Instructions for DTG Printed Garments

With proper care, DTG prints can last 50+ wash cycles while maintaining color vibrancy. Follow these guidelines:

  • Wash inside-out in cold water (30°C / 86°F max)
  • Use a gentle or delicate wash cycle
  • Use mild, non-bleach detergent
  • Tumble dry on LOW heat or hang to dry
  • Do not iron directly on the print — iron the reverse side or use a pressing cloth
  • Do not dry clean or use bleach
  • Do not wring or twist the garment

DTG vs. Screen Printing

The two most popular methods for custom t-shirt printing each have their strengths. Here's how they compare:

  • Colors: DTG handles unlimited colors at no extra cost. Screen printing charges per color (max ~12 colors).
  • Minimums: DTG has no minimums. Screen printing typically requires 12+ pieces to be economical.
  • Detail: DTG excels at photographic detail. Screen printing is best for bold, solid designs.
  • Cost at volume: Screen printing becomes cheaper per piece at 100+ units. DTG is more cost-effective for small runs.
  • Durability: Both methods produce excellent wash durability when done correctly.

When to Choose DTG Printing

Choose DTG when you need:

  • Complex artwork, photographs, or designs with gradients
  • Small quantities (1–50 pieces) without setup costs
  • Quick turnaround on one-off or sample pieces
  • Print-on-demand for e-commerce without holding inventory
  • Unlimited color designs without per-color pricing
